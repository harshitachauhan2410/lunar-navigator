# Methodology
Sun elevation at a lunar site (latitude p, longitude L):
sin(h) = sin(p) sin(d) + cos(p) cos(d) cos(L - Ls)
where d and Ls are the latitude and longitude of the point directly under the Sun.
- Ls = 180 degrees - 360 degrees x (fraction of the 29.53-day lunar cycle).
- d stays within about 1.5 degrees of the equator.
Earth uses the same formula, with its sub-Earth point moving a few degrees (libration).
The Sun counts as up when its elevation plus 0.27 degrees (solar radius) is above the horizon.
